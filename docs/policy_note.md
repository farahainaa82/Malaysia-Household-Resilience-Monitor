# Policy Note: From Nominal Growth to Household Resilience

## Decision question

Where should analysts look more closely when assessing whether Malaysian households are becoming more resilient to cost pressure?

## Why the headline income number is not enough

Household income can rise in ringgit terms while purchasing power improves more slowly. Inflation is only one part of the picture. Poverty, inequality, expenditure pressure and the labour-income environment also matter.

The purpose of this analysis is therefore not to produce a single ranking. It is to create a transparent screening view that keeps different concepts separate.

## What the monitor does

For each state, the analysis:

1. calculates nominal growth in median household income between the latest two HIES years;
2. adjusts that growth using state-level CPI;
3. retains the latest poverty rate and Gini coefficient;
4. calculates a descriptive expenditure-to-income ratio;
5. adds a separate year-on-year formal-sector median wage signal;
6. flags states where poverty is above the state median and real household income growth is below the state median.

## How the results should be used

A **higher-attention** flag should trigger a question, not a conclusion.

The next analytical step may include:
- income-distribution analysis rather than averages alone;
- household-type and urban/rural breakdowns;
- district-level variation;
- food, housing and transport price components;
- labour-force composition;
- social-transfer coverage;
- sensitivity to revised CPI or HIES estimates.

## What policymakers should not infer

The monitor does not show that inflation caused poverty, that a particular programme worked or failed, or that one state is definitively better managed than another.

Those questions require a stronger identification strategy and more granular evidence.

## Policy value

The contribution of the monitor is methodological discipline: it turns several official datasets into a reproducible decision-support view while keeping evidence boundaries visible.

That is useful because policy attention is often constrained. A transparent screening tool can help analysts decide where deeper diagnostic work is worth the time and data effort.
